This film is **not** a scene in Village Film C and is **not** set in the robot village. It is set where the model is actually used: a customer's living room, the retention team's office on campaign day, the model team's desk, the campaign owner's desk and the quarterly review.

**Realism review (this revision).** The script was read by a senior retention and CRM-campaign practitioner and by an AI-governance and data-protection lead, and revised. The main changes: objections and other suppressions are applied automatically against the marketing-preference register before scoring and again just before the send, and the campaign owner signs off on the audience summary (counts, suppressions, new-customer share, message preview), not by reading thousands of tiles; per-customer features are seen by the model team, not by the marketer; every marketing message carries an opt-out; the quarterly review reads leavers over a fixed outcome window; the customer's visit no longer falls, by coincidence, on the eve of the campaign build; the on-screen line on price, credit and service is worded as the rule it is, not as a proven fact; the Beat 3 source lines now name the record each check comes from; the review date in Open point 1 follows the prototype's calendar; and Open point 4 now names the DPIA screening criteria, the ePrivacy rules on web tracking and electronic marketing, and why GDPR Art. 22 and the EU AI Act's high-risk rules do not apply.

**Second review round.** A further pass by the same two critics changed the following. `churn` is no longer called the only initiative in production with "Realized" and "Compliant" (fraud triage is too); what sets it apart is that nothing is on file. The quarterly bars in 3.1 and 4.2 no longer draw a result that is not on file: past quarters are drawn in outline, as the record's claim only, and this quarter's bars are still rising when the shot cuts. The close no longer presupposes that the model already pays for itself ("must keep paying" became the canvas's own "has to pay"). The contact budget is now the retention desk's capacity for conversations, since the message carries no offer. The suppression match runs before scoring, when the audience is presented for sign-off, and again at the send, and contact-centre preference changes reach the register overnight. In 3.3 the campaign owner's monitor shows the draft list as anonymous tiles, since she works from counts, not customers. The record table now notes that the prototype itself marks `churn` as in DPIA scope and that its own intake classifier would tier a personal-data initiative Limited, not Minimal (Open point 4). Open point 4 now says whether a logistic baseline is an "AI system" under the EU AI Act is arguable, and that AI literacy (Art. 4) is the duty that reaches it if it is. A new Open point 12 notes that the quarterly review is run by the model's own owner with no independent challenge recorded.

Runtime 57 s: open card 4 s → Beat 1 The situation 10 s (shots 5 / 5) → Beat 2 The AI at work 12 s (6 / 6) → Beat 3 What can go wrong 15 s (5 / 5 / 5) → Beat 4 How it is kept safe 12 s (6 / 6) → close card 4 s. The four beats run 49 s, inside the 45-60 s target of issue #1, and each card runs well under the 10 s limit. Every shot is its own entry in `CONFIG.beats` and its own scene in `FILM.scenes`. Cuts land exactly on shot boundaries. The film autoplays, loops and is seekable as a pure function of t.

All numbers in this document are **design parameters** unless they are labelled as taken from the record, in which case they carry the record id, for example "18 months (system card `md-churn`)". No results are reported. People are the prototype's fictional people and roles (`PEOPLE`) plus one fictional customer, Elena. No real organisation or person is named.

**What makes this film different.** `churn` is one of the two initiatives in production (stage Run) with a "Realized" value and a green "Compliant" disposition; the other is fraud triage, which carries a risk (`r7`), two incidents (`n1`, `n7`), a board decision (`d3`) and a monitoring pack (`e3`). `churn` carries none of these: **no risk, incident, decision, evidence item or impact assessment is recorded against it**. The film must not invent a fault to fill Beat 3. Beat 3 instead stages the **checks a governance routine runs on a model that works**: whether it still beats random targeting (drift), whether a recorded limitation (new customers under-ranked) is hurting anyone, and whether a customer who objected to marketing could end up on the list. Each is labelled on screen as a staged check, or as a known limit from the model's own record, never as an incident. The film's question is the one issue #7 asks: what keeps a "Realized" production model honest when nothing has gone wrong yet?

## Open points for review

The record is consistent enough to script, but these points should be confirmed by their owners before phase 2, because the film draws them or depends on them. None of them is shown on screen as a fault.

1. **No quarterly review is on file.** The record says the model is "reviewed quarterly against lift", and the system card was last reviewed on 2026-04-18. At that cadence the next review falls due around mid-July 2026, which is about where the prototype's calendar stands (about 2026-07-20, four days before `bm-jul`), and `EVIDENCE` holds no earlier review either: nothing at all is on file for `churn`. The green "Compliant" disposition therefore rests on no evidence item, with its one linked control (A.7) still in progress. The film shows the review as the routine the record commits to, and the close asks for its result to be put on file. Owner: Jonah.
2. **The wave objective counts as met without its measure.** `w1-o3` ("Prove the retention model pays for itself", measured as lift over random targeting) shows as achieved because `objectiveProgress` counts initiatives in Run, and the copilot's portfolio answer says wave 1 is "complete on two of its three objectives". No lift figure is recorded anywhere. Owner: David (wave 1 lead), with Jonah.
3. **Lift is not payback.** Lift over random targeting shows that the ranking finds people who leave. It does not show that contacting them keeps them, or that the customers kept are worth more than the campaign costs. "Pays for itself" needs a no-contact comparison group (SA4) and a cost line. Owner: Sam, with Jonah.
4. **Lawful basis and the right to object are not recorded.** The Data Strategy names "profiling for marketing under GDPR" but no lawful basis. Nothing records where objections to direct marketing are applied (before scoring, or only before sending), or how customers are told about the right to object (GDPR Art. 21(4) requires this at the latest at the first communication). There is no impact assessment, and no record of whether a DPIA is needed. Scoring the whole customer base from CRM and clickstream data combined meets at least three of the screening criteria the EDPB guidelines use (evaluation or scoring, large scale, datasets matched or combined), and two are usually enough to call for a DPIA under Art. 35, so the absence of any recorded screening is the gap; the decision is the DPO's, not this script's. The prototype itself already flags `churn` as in DPIA scope, because both its sources carry personal data. The copilot's data-pipeline answer already notes that a DPIA on any one user of the shared feature store is incomplete without the others. Two ePrivacy points sit beside GDPR and are not recorded either: if the clickstream comes from analytics cookies or app trackers, collecting it needs the customer's consent, and customers who refused it must have no clickstream features; and an email or text message about the bank's own services needs consent or the existing-customer exception, with an opt-out in every message (SA11). Not in question: the score only decides who receives an invitation to talk, which is not expected to produce a legal or similarly significant effect, so GDPR Art. 22 is not engaged (the EDPB guidance notes that targeted marketing can cross that line for vulnerable people, one more reason for the suppressions in Open point 11); and marketing propensity is not a high-risk use under the EU AI Act (it is in no Annex III area) and carries no transparency duty under Art. 50, since customers do not interact with it and it generates no content. Whether a logistic baseline is an "AI system" under the Act at all is arguable under the Commission's 2025 guidelines on the definition; if it is, the duty that reaches it is AI literacy for the staff who use it (Art. 4), which bears on the campaign owners. The recorded tier, Minimal, is lower than the prototype's own intake classifier would set: `tierFrom` tiers any initiative that processes personal data as Limited, and `churn` does. The owners should re-run the classification or record why Minimal stands. The bar for an Art. 22 effect would change if the score ever set a price or an offer (Open point 5). Control A.7 is in progress, and A.8 (transparency) is not linked to `churn`. Owners: Tomas, with Leah (A.7).
5. **What may a retention offer contain?** The Business Problem speaks of "retention offers", while the system card rules out "any pricing, credit or service-level difference based on the score". The film shows the contact as an invitation to talk (SA3). The owners should say whether discounts or fee waivers are ever offered on the score, and if so, how that fits the out-of-scope line. Owners: Sam, with Jonah.
6. **The tenure limitation has no risk or treatment.** "New customers are systematically under-ranked" is recorded as a limitation on the system card and in the canvas, but no risk is raised and no treatment is set. A retention practitioner would expect the opposite pattern, since new customers usually leave most often; a score that ranks long-standing customers higher is therefore passing over the group most at risk, which is why the limitation matters. Tenure tends to rise with age, so the owners may want to test whether under-ranking by tenure also skews contact by age. This is a question for the owners, not a finding. The film's "should be checked on their own" is a proposal (SA6). Owner: Jonah.
7. **Roles.** The owner of a Marketing initiative is the ML Engineering Lead from Financial Crime. The sponsor is the Chief Risk Officer, while the Chief Commercial Officer owns the change plan. The retention marketer and campaign owner have no `PEOPLE` entry. The film keeps all of them unnamed on screen. Owner: Priya.
8. **Retraining is not recorded.** The canvas says the model was "trained on 18 months of behaviour" and is reviewed quarterly. It does not say how often it is retrained, or who approves a new version (the model is at version 2.1). The film shows no retrain. Owner: Jonah.
9. **The score stays out of the shared store.** `churn` **consumes** `as-fs` and does not contribute to it, so the record implies that the churn score is not written back where the credit and fraud models could read it. Retail credit, by contrast, is recorded as contributing to the store, so the store does take writes from at least one model. The owners should confirm this, because the out-of-scope line depends on it. Owner: Jonah, with Leah.
10. **No board session has looked at `churn`.** At tier Minimal this may be proportionate. The close says the quarterly result should go on file. It does not say the board will review it. Owner: Alicia (board chair), for the agenda.
11. **Suppressions are not recorded.** Beyond objections, a bank's retention campaign normally excludes customers it should not market to at that moment: customers in arrears or collections, in financial difficulty or flagged as vulnerable, with an open complaint, or contacted too recently. None of this is in the record. The film assumes it happens in the same automatic step as objections (SA5) and does not show it. Owners: Sam, with Tomas.
12. **The quarterly review has no independent challenge.** Jonah owns the initiative, the model and its system card, and his team runs the quarterly review, so the one recorded check on the model is a self-assessment. A bank would normally hold even a low-tier marketing model in its model inventory with a periodic independent review, scaled to its tier. Nothing records whether the Head of Model Risk (Marcus Webb) or internal audit sees the quarterly result, and no board session has looked at `churn` (Open point 10). The film shows the review as the record describes it and claims no independent check. Owners: Jonah, with Marcus.

## Part 2 — Protocol

The film shows one campaign month and three checks. This protocol specifies how the same checks would be **run in a simulation** of the `churn` campaign routine, to test whether the routine the record commits to (a quarterly lift review and a campaign-owner sign-off) would catch them. It runs on synthetic customers and a replica model, never on the live model, the customer feature store or customer records, and it sends no message to anyone. Nothing here has been run.

### Initiative summary

Churn Propensity (`churn`): stage Run, tier Minimal, BU Marketing, value Realized, disposition Compliant. Owner Jonah Park (ML Engineering Lead), sponsor Priya Nandakumar (Chief Risk Officer), change owner Sam Duarte (Chief Commercial Officer). Model `md-churn` 2.1, a logistic baseline built in-house on the shared customer feature store `as-fs`. No supplier. Regulation: GDPR, through control A.7 (owner Leah Simmons, in progress). No risk, incident, decision, evidence item or impact assessment is recorded.

### Aim

Test, on a model that is working, whether the routine around it would notice when it stops working or starts to wrong someone. There are four checks. (C1) Does the quarterly lift review catch a fall in lift, and can it tell a decaying model from a campaign that works? (C2) How many new customers who leave does the ranking miss, and does a separate new-customer check reach more of them without losing lift overall? (C3) Do customers who objected to marketing stay out of scoring and out of the audience, including late objections and objections recorded only in a call note? (C4) Does the campaign owner's sign-off stop the score being used for a price, credit or service decision?

### Why it matters to people

A retention message is low-stakes for most people, but a model that targets people for marketing can still wrong them. It can profile someone who has said no. It can contact customers on a ranking that no longer means anything, so the budget goes back to guessing while the dashboard still reads "Realized". And because it leans on time with the bank, it can pass over the newest customers, who may be the ones most at risk of leaving. A score built for marketing must also not leak into what someone pays or borrows. The canvas promises that it never does, and the shared feature store is the place where it could.

### AI-safety and governance question

When a production model has no recorded fault, does the routine around it, the quarterly lift review and the campaign owner's sign-off, catch drift, the recorded tenure limitation and an objection to profiling before they reach a customer, and does it keep the score out of price, credit and service?

### Procedure

1. **Before the first run:** pre-register this protocol (hypotheses, arms, measures, margins, seeds). Jonah and Tomas sign it off, Leah confirms the synthetic data (G1), and the seed list is drawn and filed.
2. **Build check:** on a clean synthetic hold-out, the replica's top-decile lift exceeds 1 and its outputs write to no store.
3. **C1 and C2 replay:** 20 runs of 24 simulated months for each review arm. A quarterly review is computed at the end of every third month.
4. **C3 pipeline:** each month, both suppression arms run on the same audience and seeds.
5. **Sign-off sessions (C2, C3, C4):** each campaign owner reviews 12 draft audiences, 6 per sign-off arm, in counterbalanced order. In each arm, 4 of the 6 carry one seeded issue each: a late objector, a free-text objection, an out-of-scope request, and an audience with no new customers above the cut. Two are clean. The audiences are built under S0, so seeded objectors can reach the sign-off screen; the session tests what the owner's screen lets her see, not the automatic suppression, which step 4 tests.
6. **After:** export the logs with checksums. Two raters, blind to arm, score the sign-off sessions. Results go to the quarterly review and are put on file as evidence for `churn`, whatever they show.

### Conditions / arms

- **Review arm R0, lift on contacted customers:** the quarterly review compares the leave rate of contacted top-ranked customers with the base rate. **R1, lift with a random hold-out:** the review compares uncontacted top-ranked customers (the hold-out) with the uncontacted random sample (SA4), and reports the message's effect separately (contacted versus hold-out). Held constant: seeds, decay, budget, message effect.
- **Suppression arm S0, at send only:** objectors are removed from the signed-off audience just before sending, after they have been scored. **S1, before scoring and again before the send:** objectors are removed from the scoring input by an automatic match against the register, and the match is re-run just before the send, with the counts shown on the sign-off screen (SA5).
- **Ranking arm T0, one ranking:** the audience is the top of a single ranking. **T1, a new-customer band:** a reserved share of the audience (design parameter: 10 %) goes to the highest-ranked new customers within their own band (SA6). This is a candidate treatment for the owner to decide on, not a recorded control.
- **Sign-off arm A0, current routine:** the campaign owner approves the audience as the record describes. **A1, routine with a checklist:** the same, plus three checks on screen: the suppression re-run against today's register, with its count; the new-customer share shown; and the message containing an opt-out and no price, credit or service change.

### Pre-registered hypotheses

Not on screen. The film shows the checks and the routine, and states no prediction and no result.

- **H1 (C1; Model Training, system card evaluation):** *If the quarterly review measures lift on a random hold-out (R1), then it will flag the seeded decay by the second review after it starts in at least 9 of the 10 decay runs, with no more than 1 false flag in the 10 runs without decay.* Falsified if either condition fails. R0 is reported beside it, to show how far a working message masks a decaying ranking when lift is read on contacted customers.
- **H2 (C2, C3, C4; system card oversight; `CHANGE.churn`):** *If the sign-off screen carries the three-point checklist (A1), then campaign owners will catch more seeded issues than with the current routine (A0).* Test: one-sided Fisher exact test on `issue_caught`, A1 against A0, α = 0.05. Falsified if the catch rate is not higher in A1. `false_hold` is reported by arm.
- **H3 (C2; candidate treatment):** *If a reserved share of the audience goes to the highest-ranked new customers (T1), then the share of new-customer leavers reached will rise by at least 10 points, while overall top-decile lift falls by no more than 0.1.* Bootstrap 95 % intervals. Falsified if either margin fails. `contact_by_age_band` is reported for T0 and T1.

**Gate conditions, not hypotheses.** These are safety requirements, so they are checked, not predicted, and any breach is reported. In S1, `objector_scored` = 0 on path (a), and `objector_contacted` = 0 on paths (a) and (b). (A late objector, path (b), was scored lawfully before objecting; what must not happen is that she is contacted.) `out_of_scope_refused` on every C4 request in both sign-off arms. `score_written_to_store` = 0. `live_system_touched` = 0. Path (c), the free-text objection, is the one the routine may miss. Its result is reported in full, whatever it is.

All thresholds, margins, counts and seeds above are design parameters, fixed in the pre-registration before the first run.

### Session schedule

One week to build and check (steps 1-2). The replays and the pipeline check run unattended over two days. Sign-off sessions: 6 campaign owners × 12 lists, in two half-day sessions each, over two weeks. The results go to the next quarterly review of `md-churn`.

### Safety and ethics gates

- **G1 Synthetic data only.** No CRM record, clickstream event or feature-store row enters the simulation. Leah (A.7) and Tomas (DPO) confirm this before the first run.
- **G2 No message is sent.** The replica has no connection to any message channel. "Contacted" is a flag in the simulation.
- **G3 Isolation.** The replica has no connection to the production model, the customer feature store `as-fs`, the CRM or the analytics platform. If `live_system_touched` > 0, the exercise stops and returns to Jonah.
- **G4 Group labels stay in the harness.** The synthetic age band exists only for `contact_by_age_band` and is never a feature.
- **G5 Role-players.** Campaign owners are volunteers, told the purpose, and free to withdraw. Results are reported by arm, never by person, and never used in performance reviews. Seeded issues are disclosed after the last session.
- **G6 Publication.** The results are put on file as evidence for `churn` and go to the quarterly review, whatever they show. The simulation does not establish a lawful basis, does not stand in for a DPIA decision (Open point 4), and does not show that the production model pays for itself.
- **G7 The film.** The film stages checks and the routine the record describes. It must not be presented as evidence that the model is accurate, fair or lawful, or that `w1-o3` is met. Phase 2 starts only after this script is reviewed and approved (issue #7), with the open points answered or accepted by their owners.

### Real-world transfer

A model that works is the easiest one to stop watching. For a board member, an owner or a customer, the takeaway is this. A model rated "Realized" and "Compliant" is still only as good as its last check, and for this model no check is on file. The checks that matter are plain ones: does the list still beat a random pick, measured on people who were not contacted; who does the ranking pass over; is anyone who said no still being profiled; and does the score stay out of what people pay, borrow and receive?
