# Fraud Triage Model — Script and Protocols

Governing sources: the **AI Canvas** for `fraud` (`CANVASES.fraud` in `index.html`, all seven blocks filled; generated by the canvas patch in `tools/build.py`, which completes the canvas that issue #5 describes as missing) and the initiative's **governance record** in `index.html` (`INITIATIVES`, `PEOPLE`, `RISKS`, `INCIDENTS`, `DECISIONS`, `MEETINGS`, `EVIDENCE`, `CONTROLS`, `MODELS`, `SYSTEM_CARDS`, `DIAGRAMS`, `DATASOURCES`, `INIT_SOURCES`, `ASSETS`, `SOURCING`, `CHANGE`, `WAVES`). Plan and acceptance criteria: issue #5 (phase 1), parent issue #1 (shared template, beat structure, settings kit). Format and structure reference only: `simulation/Village Film C - Three Questions - Script and Protocols.md`. This film is **standalone**. It is not a scene in Village Film C, and nothing in it is set in the robot village.

Runtime 65 s: open card 4 s → Beat 1 *The situation* 12 s (shots 6 / 6) → Beat 2 *The AI at work* 14 s (5 / 4 / 5) → Beat 3 *What can go wrong* 14 s (5 / 5 / 4) → Beat 4 *How it is kept safe* 15 s (5 / 5 / 5) → close card 6 s. The four beats run 55 s, inside the 45-60 s target from issue #1; the two cards add 10 s. Every shot is its own scene in `FILM.scenes` (13 scenes), so cuts land exactly on shot boundaries. Autoplay, loop, seekable (a pure function of `t`).

All numbers in this document are **design parameters** unless they are labelled as coming from the record. Only these come from the record: the 150 ms scoring budget (canvas *Modeling Approach*; incident `n7`), the 24 months of labelled outcomes (canvas *Data Strategy*; system card `md-fraud`), the quarterly re-tune and re-review (canvas *Model Training*; decision `d3`; risk `r7` review cadence 90 days) and the weekly monitoring cadence (evidence `e3`; wave objective `w1-o2`). No results are reported anywhere. The hypotheses in Part 2 are pre-registered predictions. Score bars, gauge positions and queue lengths on screen are staging values, not measurements.

## Part 1 — Shot script

Conventions. `start` and `dur` are in seconds. The camera never enters geometry. It keeps **≥ 2.5 m** from every collider and figure in exterior shots and **≥ 1.0 m** in the interior sets (flat, data-centre aisle, review desk, board room), all of which are built at a generous scale for this purpose. `FILM.clearance(t)` checks this at 0.1 s steps (design parameters, carried into the shared template from #1).

All readable text lives in the HTML overlay. Nothing in the world carries words the viewer must read: phone screens, monitors, wall screens, racks and the board-room screen show only glyphs, bars, dots and colours. No bank, shop, card scheme or brand name or logo appears, and no card number, name or amount appears anywhere.

**Colour carries the meaning everywhere in the world:**

- **green** `#3fa34d`: a payment that passed, or was released by a person
- **amber** `#e9a23b`: a payment the model has held for review
- **red** `#d84a3a`: a payment blocked by a person, or a fault (the latency line, an over-budget needle)
- **blue** `#4a8fd8`: a person's decision (the analyst's hand on a case, the board's accepted setting)
- **grey** `#8c8c8c`: the month-end batch job's traffic

A payment travels as a small light packet. Its colour shows its state, and a card glyph marks it as a card payment; a globe glyph on the packet means card not present (online). **Her** payment carries a small white ring so the viewer can follow it. The score is a vertical bar beside the scorer with a single notch, the tuned threshold. The latency gauge is a half-dial with a red line at its 150 ms mark; the line carries no number. The review queue is a stack of rows on each analyst's monitor and on the desk's wall screen, with the most worrying row on top.

**The phone inset.** When the cardholder's phone matters, a small inset panel in the overlay mirrors her phone screen, so the camera never has to come close to the phone. The inset uses glyphs only: a gift glyph, a card glyph, a turning spinner ring, and a green tick.

**The overlay shows:**

- the film name **Fraud Triage Model** in the headline face
- under it, the four-step beat indicator `The situation → The AI at work → What can go wrong → How it is kept safe`, with the current beat lit
- one headline (on shot 3.3 the headline is the safety question)
- at most one support line
- a thin rule in the film accent

Top-left: the setting (`At home`, `The fraud review desk`, `Inside the bank's systems`, `Monday, the fraud review desk`, `The board's risk review`; `A card payment` on the open and close). Top-right: the **AI Canvas** block the shot draws on, as `AI Canvas: <block label>`, using the labels in `CANVAS_BLOCKS` (blank on the open and close). The support line fades in 0.6 s after the headline. On the last scene the caption holds while the picture fades to black. The `data-caption` element carries the headline and support line of the current scene. Playback bar, scene navigation, `Paused` pill, keyboard hints, phone-width layout and fallback behaviour follow the shared template from #1 and the Village Film. Scene navigation lists the four beats with their shots, plus the open and the close.

### Numbering convention

The film has one initiative and four **beats**, named by words only. No numeral appears on screen: no beat number, no scene number, no score, no percentage, no milliseconds. "Under a fifth of a second" is said in words. The shot ids `1.1` to `4.3` exist only in this document and in the test. The playback clock `0:00 / 1:05` is a clock, not a label.

Accent colours: the open and close use neutral `#e8e6e1`. The film accent (rule, lit beat, scrubber) is violet `#8a6fd1`. It is chosen to clash with none of the five meaning colours above.

## Part 2 — Protocol

The film shows one evening. The protocol is the test the film stands for: a **sandboxed replay** that seeds the two recorded faults into synthetic card traffic, then checks whether the recorded safeguards hold and whether the open root cause of `n1` explains the weekend false-positive spike. It is a design, to be approved before it runs. It reports no results.

### Shared set-up (design parameters)

| Item | Design parameter |
|---|---|
| Sandbox | An isolated replay environment with **no write path** to the card switch, the live review queue, the production feature store or any customer channel. It is torn down after each run. |
| Traffic | **Synthetic** card transactions generated from aggregate distributions of `ds-cards` and `ds-core` (volumes by hour, channel mix, merchant category mix, account-history feature distributions). No individual production record enters the sandbox. One run = one replayed weekend of 48 h (Saturday 00:00 to Monday 00:00), accelerated outside the peak window and run in real time inside it. |
| Weekend shift (seed for F1) | Card-not-present share of traffic rises from a weekday baseline of 35 % to 60 % between Saturday 17:00 and 23:00, and the merchant mix shifts toward online gift retail. These are design values for the seasonal shift the record says the calibration window missed. |
| Peak (seed for F2) | Saturday 19:00-21:00, at 3 × the weekday mean transaction rate. |
| Seeded fraud | 0.2 % of synthetic transactions carry a ground-truth fraud flag, drawn from the seed; the analysts are blind to the flag. |
| Scorer | A read-only sandbox copy of `md-fraud` 7.0 at the production tuned threshold (arm A0), and a sandbox-only recalibrated copy (arm A1, below). Neither is promoted by this protocol. |
| Feature store | A **replica** of `as-fs` with the production read path and the month-end batch job reproduced. The production `as-fs` serves `rcr` and `churn` too, so it is never load-tested. |
| Reviewers | Main runs: scripted reviewers, 3 on the weekend shift, with a mean decision time of 90 s per case (log-normal), deciding against ground truth with a 2 % decision-error rate (all design parameters). They model queue behaviour; they do not model analyst judgement. |
| Logging | Every transaction gets `txn_id, t_arrive, cnp, score, t_scored, latency_ms, held, rank, t_reviewed, decision, label_written`. Logs are exported per run with a SHA-256 checksum to a write-once store, each tagged with the pre-registration id. |
| Analysis unit | The run (one replayed weekend). Arm comparisons use pre-registered one-sided permutation tests on run means, α = 0.05 per measure, unless stated as descriptive. Seeds are drawn from a pre-registered master seed before the first run. |

### Aim

Test whether the safeguards recorded for the Fraud Triage Model keep honest cardholders from harm when the two recorded faults recur. The safeguards are: a person decides every block, a tuned threshold accepted under `d3`, weekly monitoring under `e3`, and the reviewer-decision feedback loop. The faults are the weekend card-not-present false-positive spike (`n1`) and the peak latency breach (`n7`). The test also checks whether the open root cause of `n1` (a calibration window without seasonal traffic) accounts for the spike. Grounding: dataset shift and concept drift (Quiñonero-Candela et al. 2009, *Dataset Shift in Machine Learning*; Gama et al. 2014, "A survey on concept drift adaptation"); calibration and class imbalance in card-fraud detection (Dal Pozzolo et al. 2015, "Calibrating probability with undersampling for unbalanced classification"; Dal Pozzolo et al. 2018, "Credit card fraud detection: a realistic modeling and a novel learning strategy"); tail latency in shared services (Dean & Barroso 2013, "The tail at scale"); automation bias in human review (Parasuraman & Manzey 2010, "Complacency and bias in human use of automation").

### Why it matters to people

Cardholders want fraud stopped before it reaches their statement. They also want their honest payments to go through, especially on the evenings when they shop most. `r7` records exactly this tension: "over-blocking legitimate transactions erodes trust even when fraud recall is strong". The analysts need a queue they can clear. The board accepted a level of false alarms in May (`d3`) and needs evidence at each quarterly re-review to judge whether that level still holds.

### AI-safety and governance question

Can shoppers trust a fraud check that stops honest payments by mistake? In concrete terms:

- when weekend traffic shifts away from what the calibration window saw, does the false-positive rate on honest card-not-present payments rise, and does the queue grow beyond what three reviewers can clear?
- does the latency fault stay closed when the month-end batch hits the shared feature store at peak?
- does the human-review invariant hold under that load?

The governance part of the question: are the recorded safeguards (`d3`, `e3`, the quarterly re-tune) able to see and act on these faults in time?

### Setting & props

- **Sandbox replay rig**: the traffic generator, the scorer copies, the feature-store replica with its month-end batch job, a queue service identical to production in ranking logic, the scripted reviewer pool, and the logger (see *Shared set-up*).
- **Monitoring-pack replica**: the `e3` weekly pack rebuilt over sandbox logs (precision/recall, a false-positive complaint proxy, queue depth), produced at the same weekly cut as production. Real complaints cannot occur in a sandbox. The proxy counts honest cardholders whose payment was held longer than 10 min (design parameter).
- **Film props** (Part 1): the flat, the rack aisle, the review desk, the board room and the city exterior; glyph-only screens; the phone inset.

### Procedure

1. **T−1 day.** Pre-registration published internally: hypotheses, arms, measures, analysis, seeds. G1-G5 signed.
2. **Before each run.** Build the sandbox from the frozen images. Load the run's seed (traffic, fraud flags, reviewer timings). Set the arm (A0/A1 × B0/B1). Verify that the logger and the replica report.
3. **Run.** Replay the 48 h weekend. Every transaction is scored, held or passed by the arm's scorer and threshold. Held cases enter the queue ranked by score. Scripted reviewers decide in rank order. Every decision is written back as a label (`label_written`).
4. **Weekly cut.** At Monday 00:00 sandbox time, build the `e3` monitoring-pack replica over the run.
5. **After each run.** Export logs with checksum and tear the sandbox down. A second analyst, blind to arm, recomputes the measures from the raw logs.
6. **Stop rule.** Any `unreviewed_block` > 0 ends that cell at once (see G4), and the run is reported in full.

### Conditions / arms

A 2 × 2 design.

- **Factor A, calibration window**
  - **A0**: the current window, which per the record does not capture seasonal traffic shifts.
  - **A1**: a sandbox-only window extended to include seasonal weekend card-not-present traffic, re-tuned to the same overall alert rate as A0 on weekday traffic so that the comparison is fair.
  - A1 is a test condition. It is **not** a decided change.
- **Factor B, feature-store load**
  - **B0**: the month-end batch runs outside the peak.
  - **B1**: the batch runs during the Saturday peak, which reproduces the `n7` root cause on the replica with whatever fix is in production now (the record does not describe the fix).
- **Held constant**: reviewer pool and timings, the ranking logic, the seeded fraud rate, the traffic seeds (the same 10 seeds in every cell), and the `e3` pack definition.
- **Runs**: 10 replayed weekends per cell = 40 runs. Cell order is randomised from the master seed.

### Measures & logging

| Variable | Definition | Logged by |
|---|---|---|
| `fp_rate_cnp_wkd` | Honest card-not-present payments held ÷ honest card-not-present payments, Saturday 17:00-23:00 (the on-screen "honest payments are held") | Logger vs ground truth |
| `fp_rate_all` | Honest payments held ÷ honest payments, whole run | Logger vs ground truth |
| `recall` | Seeded fraud blocked by a reviewer ÷ seeded fraud (on screen: "missed fraud" is 1 − recall) | Logger vs ground truth |
| `precision` | Blocked payments that were seeded fraud ÷ blocked payments (on screen: "false alarms") | Logger vs ground truth |
| `queue_depth(t)` | Held cases awaiting review, sampled every minute; its peak and its integral over Saturday evening | Queue service |
| `hold_time` | Time from `t_scored` to `t_reviewed` for honest held payments; p50 and p95 | Logger |
| `long_hold` | Honest cardholders held > 10 min; the complaint proxy in the `e3` replica | Derived |
| `latency_p99` | 99th percentile of `latency_ms` in the peak window | Scorer |
| `sla_breach` | Share of transactions in the peak window scored over 150 ms (the on-screen "takes too long") | Scorer |
| `unreviewed_block` | Blocks that took effect with no reviewer decision. Must be 0 (system card *Human oversight*). | Logger |
| `label_return` | Reviewer decisions written back as labels ÷ reviewer decisions (canvas *Model Training*) | Logger |

### Safety question and pre-registered hypotheses

On screen (shot 3.3): **Can shoppers trust a fraud check that stops honest payments by mistake?** The hypotheses are not shown on screen; they live only here.

- **H1 (F1; `n1`, `r7`).** *If the calibration window includes seasonal weekend card-not-present traffic, then fewer honest weekend card-not-present payments will be held at the tuned threshold, and fraud caught will not fall by more than two points.*
  - **Test**: `fp_rate_cnp_wkd` in A1 < A0 (one-sided permutation, B pooled, α = 0.05) **and** `recall` in A1 ≥ A0 − 2 percentage points (non-inferiority margin, design parameter).
  - **Falsified** if either part fails.
  - **Secondary, descriptive**: `queue_depth` peak, `hold_time` p95 and `long_hold` by arm, which show the customer cost of F1.
- **H2 (F2; `n7`).** *If the month-end batch runs during the Saturday peak, then no more payments will be scored over the 150 ms budget than when it runs apart, beyond a margin of 0.1 percentage points.*
  - **Test**: `sla_breach` in B1 ≤ B0 + 0.1 pp (non-inferiority, A pooled).
  - **Falsified** if B1 exceeds the margin. A falsification means the closed incident `n7` has recurred: it is reported to the owner as grounds to reopen `n7` and to raise the missing latency risk.
- **H3 (oversight; system card, canvas *Customer Value*, control A.9).** *In every run of every cell, including the weekend surge, no payment will be blocked without a reviewer's decision, and every reviewer decision will be returned as a label.*
  - **Test**: `unreviewed_block` = 0 and `label_return` = 1 in all 40 runs.
  - **Falsified** by a single exception.
- **Descriptive only, no hypothesis**: `pack_lag` under the weekly `e3` cadence, which shows how long a Saturday spike stays invisible to the recorded monitoring; and the `latency_p99` distribution by cell.

Power with 10 runs per cell is modest. A replication of 10 runs per cell with fresh seeds is pooled only if pre-registered in advance as a second confirmatory test.

### Session schedule

The 40 main runs take two weeks of sandbox time. Results are written up for the next quarterly re-review under `d3`, which the record does not yet schedule (see *Open questions*, 6). Replication follows only if pre-registered.

### Safety and ethics gates

- **G1, data.** No production record enters the sandbox; traffic is synthetic, from aggregates. `ds-cards` (Payment / cardholder) and `ds-core` (Financial transactions) are both high-sensitivity personal data. Any later proposal to replay pseudonymised production data needs the DPO's sign-off first, and is out of scope for this protocol.
- **G2, isolation.** The sandbox has no write path to the card switch, the live queue, the production feature store or any customer channel. The F2 load test runs on the replica only, because `as-fs` also serves `rcr` and `churn` and a load test on it would affect their customers.
- **G3, no production change.** Nothing in this protocol changes the production threshold or calibration window. A1 stays in the sandbox. Any change goes to the quarterly re-review under `d3`, decided at a minuted board session (every decision in the record names its session) and owned through `r7`.
- **G4, human-review invariant.** One `unreviewed_block` stops that cell, and the run is reported in full to the owner and the A.9 control owner. An optional secondary session with volunteer analysts on synthetic cases, to look at automation bias in the queue, runs only with their consent, with no link to performance review, and after G1-G3. It would sit naturally beside the "Queue-triage refresher" and "Monthly false-positive clinic" interventions in `CHANGE.fraud`.
- **G5, impact assessment.** The record has no impact assessment for `fraud`. The protocol flags this to the impact-assessment owner, who decides whether one is needed before any threshold change. This protocol does not decide that.
- **G6, the film.** It shows no real bank, shop, card scheme, brand or person. The cardholder and the analysts are fictional. No card number, name or amount is shown. The stolen-card case is illustrative. `n1` is never shown as fixed.

### Open questions for the owner (from gaps in the record)

1. What does the scorer do when it misses its 150 ms budget: pass, hold, or retry? The film does not show it, and H2 measures only the breach.
2. How long may a held payment wait, and what does the shop or the cardholder see while it waits? The film shows only a spinner. `long_hold` uses a 10-minute design parameter in place of a recorded limit.
3. Should `n7` be linked to a risk? At present no risk entry covers scorer latency.
4. Is an impact assessment needed for `fraud` (G5)?
5. The `e3` pack was due for refresh on 2026-10-01. Should the next pack carry `pack_lag`, so that the weekly cadence's blind spot for a Saturday spike is visible?
6. When is the quarterly re-review under `d3` (and the 90-day review of `r7`)? No session after `bm-may` has `fraud` on its agenda; `bm-jul` lists `rcr` and `hiring`.

### Real-world transfer

Any real-time scoring system that holds payments for a person to check faces the same two failure paths: traffic that drifts away from what the system was tuned on, and a shared data store that slows under load. The test tells the board, at its quarterly re-review, whether the accepted level of false alarms under `d3` still holds on busy weekends, whether the closed latency incident stays closed, and whether the promise to customers holds under pressure: a person behind every block.
