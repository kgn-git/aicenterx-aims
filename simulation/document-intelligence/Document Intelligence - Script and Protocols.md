# Document Intelligence — Script and Protocols

Governing sources: the AI Canvas for `docai` in `CANVASES` and the initiative's governance record in `index.html` (`INITIATIVES`, `PEOPLE`, `RISKS`, `INCIDENTS`, `CONTROLS`, `EVIDENCE`, `DECISIONS`, `MEETINGS`, `IMPACTS`, `MODELS`, `SYSTEM_CARDS`, `DIAGRAMS`, `DATASOURCES` / `INIT_SOURCES`, `DATA_SOURCE_CATEGORIES`, `ASSETS`, `SUPPLIERS`, `SOURCING`, `CHANGE`, `BUDGETS`, `WAVES`, `REGS`), read as data and not changed. `index.html` is generated by `tools/build.py`; the canvas text is set there and copied here verbatim. Plan and acceptance criteria: GitHub issue #8 (Document Intelligence: AI Canvas, narrative script and simulation), parent issue #1 (shared approach, beat structure, runtime target). Technical contract and script structure: `simulation/_template/README.md`. Format reference only: `simulation/Village Film C - Three Questions - Script and Protocols.md`. This film is **not** a scene in Village Film C and is **not** set in the robot village. It is set where the service would be used: a back office where scanned documents are keyed into the record, the pipeline that would send the scans to the vendor, a test bench where the vendor service was evaluated, and the risk office where the supplier is reviewed.

Runtime 56 s: open card 4 s → Beat 1 The situation 8 s (shot 1.1) → Beat 2 The AI at work 12 s (6 / 6) → Beat 3 What can go wrong 12 s (6 / 6) → Beat 4 How it is kept safe 16 s (5.5 / 5.5 / 5) → close card 4 s. The four beats run 48 s, inside the 45-60 s target of issue #1; the two cards add 8 s, each under the 10 s card limit. Every shot is its own scene in `FILM.scenes`; cuts land exactly on shot boundaries. The film autoplays, loops and is seekable as a pure function of t.

All numbers in this document are **design parameters** unless they are labelled as taken from the record (they then carry the record id, for example "500-document sample (system card `md-ocr`)"). No results are reported. The film stages what the governance record holds (one risk, one closed incident, the system card, the supplier record, the change plan and the canvas) and the controls the canvas commits to; the hypotheses in Part 2 are pre-registered predictions for a simulation that has not been run. People are the prototype's fictional people and roles (`PEOPLE`), plus one fictional customer. No real organisation or person is named; the vendor's name from the record is never shown on screen.

## Open points for review

Writing a realistic usage story from this record exposed the gaps below. The script stages around them and says so; the owners should confirm or correct them before phase 2, because the film draws them.

1. **The system card does not record the human check.** The canvas (Model Training, Customer Value), the sourcing rationale ("the review step kept") and the diagram ("Back-office check") all say a checker confirms every extraction, but the `md-ocr` card's oversight field is empty. The film shows the check as the plan. Owner: Leah, with Maria (A.9).
2. **The data source is classed as not personal, and that is wrong for these scans.** The canvas says "scans can name people, so GDPR applies", and the initiative lists `gdpr`; but `ds-scans` sits in the category "Images / media", whose profile says no personal data. A signed customer contract identifies the customer, so it is personal data (GDPR Art. 4(1)); the category profile cannot settle that, and it also feeds the risk tier (still "Needs review"). No impact assessment exists for `docai`. The film treats the scans as personal data (the customer's contract). What is open is the follow-through, not the classification: recording `ds-scans` as personal, and a DPIA screening under A.5 that records whether a full DPIA (Art. 35) is needed. Owner: Tomas (A.5), with Leah (A.7).
3. **Residency is "confirmed" but no evidence is filed.** The supplier note and the summary say residency is confirmed; unlike the forecasting supplier (e4), `sup-dp` has no supplier assessment in `EVIDENCE`. The film says "confirmed", as the record does. Owner: Priya (A.10).
4. **How n3 was closed.** n3 is closed, but the record does not say whether the vendor changed the default, our connector settings were changed, or long documents are now split; nor whether the 500-document evaluation was re-run afterwards and how many multi-page contracts the sample holds. The film says only "logged" and "closed". The root cause also names "the vendor connector" while the pipeline (`as-pipe`) is ours: who owns that setting decides who can change it again. Owner: Leah, with Priya.
5. **r8 has no treatment and no decision**, and its likelihood is "unlikely" although n3, the failure it describes, has already happened once in testing. The only safeguard staged against it is a trained person; a check that does not depend on attention (for example, reconciling the pages scanned with the pages the service returned fields for, arm K2) is not recorded anywhere. r8's harm is filed as `transparency` (contestation); a wrong or missing field about a customer also engages the accuracy principle (GDPR Art. 5(1)(d)) and his right to rectification (Art. 16). The film shows r8 "under review" and the pilot waiting on it. Owner: Leah.
6. **No gate owner.** No board session has reviewed `docai`, and no decision names it. The film shows the next gate as an empty decision slot. Owner: David (sponsor), with Leah.
7. **The safeguard is a people process, and it is the least funded part.** Every extraction is checked by a person, yet the people-process budget is 26 % of the total (target 70 %) with 45 of 200 spent, and ADKAR ability is 20. The value measure (`w2-o2`, documents "extracted without rekeying") also does not count checking time, so Business Value depends on checking being faster than keying. A rushed check that passes fields without looking is not recorded as a risk; the film does not stage it as a fault, and the protocol measures it (H2, H4). Owner: David (change owner), with Leah.
8. **Out-of-scope boundary.** The system card puts "any contract interpretation" out of scope. The film shows fields being extracted from contracts and never shows a reading of what a contract means. Owner: Leah.
9. **What the vendor may do with our scans is not recorded.** "Residency confirmed" covers where the scans are processed, not what the vendor may do with them. The canvas says the vendor trains its model "and we cannot see on what"; nothing on file says whether the supplier terms forbid training on our scans, which sub-processors see them, or how long they are kept. As processor of personal data the vendor needs written terms (GDPR Art. 28) that cover these points. The organisation is a bank (the sponsor is COO, Retail Banking), so a governance lead would also expect this SaaS arrangement to be recorded as an ICT third-party service under DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, applicable since 17 January 2025): an entry in the register of information (Art. 28) and the contract terms of Art. 30, plus an assessment under the EBA outsourcing guidelines of whether the arrangement is outsourcing and whether it supports a critical or important function, which sets how strict those terms must be. The record lists neither DORA nor any outsourcing assessment for `sup-dp`. The film does not show or claim any of this. Owner: Priya (A.10), with Tomas.
10. **Confidence scores and where the checker looks.** Extraction services of this kind usually return a confidence score per field, and checking screens often highlight low-confidence fields. The record does not say whether DocuParse returns them or whether the check would use them. If it did, checkers would be steered towards flagged fields; a field on a page that was never read has no score at all, so it would draw no attention (the n3 pattern). The film shows no scores (SA15), and the protocol holds them off in every arm. Owner: Leah, with Maria (A.9).

**Regulatory position as the film states it.** The record lists `gdpr` and `iso-42001` for `docai`, not `eu-ai-act`. Extracting fields from back-office documents is not one of the high-risk uses listed in Annex III of the EU AI Act; if extracted fields later feed a credit decision, that decision system carries its own duties. The one AI Act duty that does reach this deployment is AI literacy (Art. 4, applicable to every deployer since 2 February 2025); the check-step walkthrough is where it would be evidenced, and the record does not link the two. GDPR Art. 22 is not engaged because the service takes no decision about anyone: it transcribes fields. That holds whatever the check does, so the film never presents the human check as an Art. 22 safeguard. What the check serves under GDPR is the accuracy of personal data (Art. 5(1)(d)) and the customer's right to have it corrected (Art. 16); see Open point 5. For the vendor arrangement under GDPR Art. 28 and DORA, see Open point 9. The film therefore makes no AI Act or DORA claim and states no legal duty on screen; the controls it shows are the organisation's own (ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A controls A.7, A.9, A.10 and the change plan), described as planned or in progress.

## Part 1 — Shot script

### Conventions

`start` and `dur` are in seconds. The camera never enters geometry. Indoors (back office, test bench, risk office) it keeps at least **1.0 m** from every collider and every person (`CONFIG.minClearance`, the template default for indoor sets); in the abstract region set of shot 2.1 it keeps at least **2.5 m** from every object. Both are design parameters checked by `FILM.clearance(t)` at 0.1 s steps. Rooms are built with `room({...})` with an open front, so the camera can look in from outside. Human figures are the template's low-poly, faceless people.

**Overlay.** The overlay holds the only readable text, as in section 1.5 of the template README. Allowed overlay elements in this film:

| Element | Content in this film |
|---|---|
| Setting label, top left | The place and moment, from the `setting` column below, on a small pill with a dot in the beat accent. No digits. |
| Initiative label, top right | `Document Intelligence`, the same on every scene, including the open and close cards. **Allowed overlay extra.** |
| Beat numeral | The large numeral 1-4 in the beat accent, beside the source line. Hidden on the cards. **Allowed overlay extra.** |
| Source line | The small line beside the numeral naming the canvas block or record the shot traces to, exactly as in the `source line` column. Beats 1-4 only. **Allowed overlay extra.** |
| Beat indicator | `The situation → The AI at work → What can go wrong → How it is kept safe`, current beat lit; at phone width the current beat in words and the others as dots. Hidden on the cards. |
| Caption (`[data-caption]`) | One headline and one support line per scene, exactly as below. This film uses **no notes, chips, cards or stages**. |
| Navigation rail | One **numbered dot** per beat group plus the list button; the list shows Introduction, the four beats with their shots by setting, and Close. **Allowed overlay extra.** |
| Playback bar, `Paused` pill, keyboard hints, fallback | As the template implements them, unchanged. |

The four elements marked **allowed overlay extra** (the initiative label top right, the beat numeral, the numbered navigation dots and the source line) are the template's own overlay and are used as the template builds them, with no template change. Every source line names an AI Canvas block for `docai` or a record id that exists in `index.html` (`n3`, `r8`, `sup-dp`, `CHANGE.docai`); the `traces to` column of the shot table gives the field each one rests on. A source line never names a record that does not exist, such as a decision, evidence item or board session for `docai`.

**Fallback title** (`CONFIG.fallbackTitle`): `A bought service could read our scans.` Then the template's fallback note and every scene in words.

**Accents** (template defaults, kept): cards neutral `#e8e6e1`; 1 The situation teal `#2a9d8f`; 2 The AI at work blue `#4a86d8`; 3 What can go wrong red `#d84a3a`; 4 How it is kept safe green `#3fa34d`. Pale tints as in `CONFIG.accentLight`.

**In-world screens show glyphs, bars, rows and colours only, never words.** The colour language is the template's, applied to this film:

| Colour or form | Meaning in this film |
|---|---|
| **Blue** | The AI or its data: scan packets in the pipeline, the layout boxes drawn on a scan, extracted field rows not yet checked |

**Glyph vocabulary** (in-world, no words): a page with grey strokes (a scan page), a squiggle on the last page (a signature), a small person glyph on a cover or drawer tag (a customer's document), field rows (strokes in a form), a dashed box (a field located on the scan), a tick (confirmed), a pen over a row (corrected), a thumbnail strip along the scan viewer (every page of the document), a ring clamp with a plug (the vendor connector), a map pin in a ring (where data is processed), a page stack with a broken bar (the page-limit defect), a closed folder with a tick (incident closed), an hourglass (under review), an outlined empty slot (nothing recorded yet), a crossed-out eye (cannot be inspected), a crossed-out keyboard (saved without rekeying), a barrier gate with a lamp (the pilot gate).

Digits appear on screen only in fixed overlay furniture (the beat numeral, the numbered rail dots, the time readout `0:00 / 0:56`) and in the record ids that the source line names (`n3`, `r8`).

## Part 2 — Protocol

The film shows the planned check once and the one recorded fault. This protocol specifies how that fault, and the risk behind it, would be **staged in a simulation** of the `docai` intake path before any pilot, to test whether the control the canvas commits to (a person checks every extracted field before it is saved) catches them, and at what cost in time. It runs on synthetic documents and a mock record, never on real scans or the real record of decision. Its findings would feed the r8 review that Leah owns; they would not replace it. Nothing here has been run.

### Initiative summary

Stage Discovery; tier Needs review; BU Operations; owner Leah Simmons (Head of Data Science); sponsor and change owner David Chen (COO, Retail Banking); model `md-ocr` (DocuParse OCR + layout, vendor version, candidate, deviating from the reference stack); supplier `sup-dp` (DocuParse, buy-saas, posture Medium, control A.10 held by Priya Nandakumar); data `ds-scans` via `as-pipe`; risk r8 (under review, no treatment); incident n3 (closed); no decision, no evidence, no impact assessment, no board review. The AI Canvas is complete and quoted verbatim above.

### Aim

Test, before a pilot decision, whether the back-office check stops the two failure kinds in the record from reaching the record: (1) silent truncation of multi-page documents (as at n3); (2) unflagged field errors in general (r8). In addition, test whether the count of corrections is a fair measure of the service's accuracy (the canvas's only accuracy check), and whether checking is faster than keying (the condition under which Business Value holds).

### Why it matters to people

Whatever is in the record is what later decisions about a customer rest on. A field typed wrong by hand is a known problem; the canvas promises to reduce it. A field the service never read is a new problem: it does not look wrong, it is simply absent, and the service gave no warning when it happened (n3). Once such a gap is saved, nothing shows it, and the customer has nothing to challenge (r8). The checker is the only point where it can be caught, and she is also the person whose work the service is meant to speed up.

### AI-safety and governance question

When a bought model we cannot inspect hands back fields that look complete, does the person who checks them catch what is missing or wrong before it is saved, and does the count of her fixes tell us honestly how good the model is, without costing more time than keying the document by hand?

### Setting and props (simulation)

- **Synthetic document set.** Contracts and back-office forms generated for the exercise, with fictional names, addresses and values; a known ground truth for every field. Multi-page contracts are included at a share set by the team (design parameter: 10 of 40 documents per checker are multi-page contracts of 6 to 14 pages).
- **Vendor outputs.** The synthetic documents are run once through the vendor's service, under the existing evaluation arrangement and only if Priya confirms the supplier terms allow it (G2), and the outputs are recorded. If the terms do not allow it, outputs are produced by an in-house stand-in extractor and the report says so.
- **Fault harness.** A small program between the recorded outputs and the checking screen that seeds the faults below. It never re-creates the page-limit default on the vendor's side.
- **Checking screen mock-up.** The scan viewer (with a thumbnail strip) beside the form, as in shots 2.2, 3.2 and 4.1, with confirm, correct and hold actions, logging every action with a timestamp. The form shows a row for every field in the document type's template, filled or blank (SA11), and the record's normal mandatory-field validation runs on save, so the exercise tests only the gaps that validation cannot see. No per-field confidence score is shown in any arm (Open point 10), so every arm tests the check the canvas describes: every field, confirmed by a person.
- **Mock record.** A sandbox copy of the record schema, with no connection to the real record of decision or to any system that reads it.
- **Keying baseline.** The current manual keying screen, for a subset of documents.

### Procedure

1. **Before the first run:** pre-register this protocol (hypotheses, arms, measures, margins, seeds, document set) with Marcus; Leah signs it off as owner. Gates G1 (Tomas) and G2 (Priya) are confirmed in writing.
2. **Build check:** on a clean copy of the document set (no seeded faults), the harness passes every field through unchanged and the mock record matches the ground truth.
3. **Assignment:** volunteer checkers are randomised to an arm (design parameter: 8 per arm). Each checks the same 40 synthetic documents in a random order, with the seeded faults placed by seed. Checkers are told the exercise measures checking quality and time; they are not told that faults are seeded, which kinds, or how many, so that they check at their normal pace.
4. **Walkthrough:** arm K1 (and K2) receive the check-step walkthrough before their session; arm K0 receives it after the exercise, so no one is left without it.
5. **Keying baseline:** every checker also keys 5 matched documents by hand, timed, in a random position in the session.
6. **Throughout:** the mock record logs every save; every seeded fault is tracked to whether it was caught before save.
7. **After:** logs are exported with checksums; results are written up for the r8 review and for whoever is named to take the pilot gate (Open point 6), whatever they show.

### Conditions / arms

- **Arm K0, check as today:** the checker confirms the form beside the scan viewer, with no walkthrough. Held constant: documents, seeds, screen.
- **Arm K1, check after the walkthrough:** the same screen, after the check-step walkthrough (step through every page, compare every field). This is the control the canvas and change plan describe.
- **Arm K2, exploratory, not in the record:** as K1, plus one line on the checking screen showing the number of pages scanned beside the number of pages the service returned fields for. This is a **candidate treatment for r8**, offered to the r8 review because r8 has no treatment yet. No hypothesis is attached to it; it is reported descriptively.

### Pre-registered hypotheses

Not on screen: the film shows the recorded fault and the planned control, and states no prediction and no result.

- **H1 (F1, n3):** If checkers have had the walkthrough (K1), then they will catch truncated contracts more often than checkers who have not (K0), and in K1 at least 38 of the 40 seeded truncated contracts (8 checkers × 5) are caught before save. Test: one-sided Fisher exact test on `truncation_caught`, K1 vs K0, α = 0.05, with a checker-clustered bootstrap as a check. **Falsified if K1 is not higher or fewer than 38 are caught in K1.**
- **H2 (F2, r8):** If checkers have had the walkthrough (K1), then `errors_saved_per_1000` will be lower than in K0, and `false_correction_per_1000` will be no more than 1 higher than in K0. Test: checker-clustered bootstrap, 95 % intervals. **Falsified if either condition fails.**
- **H3 (feedback loop, Model Training):** In K1, `accuracy_from_fixes` lies within ±2 percentage points of `accuracy_true`. Reported for K0 as well, where missed errors are expected to make the count look better than the truth. **Falsified if K1 falls outside the margin.** If falsified, the canvas's "corrections are our accuracy check" needs a second measure.
- **H4 (value, Business Value and `w2-o2`):** In K1, the median `check_time_s` per document is lower than the median `key_time_s` for matched documents. **Falsified if it is not lower.** If falsified, the result goes to David as change owner, because the case for faster intake then rests on something other than checking.

All thresholds, margins, counts and seeds above are design parameters, fixed in the pre-registration before the first run.

### Safety and ethics gates

- **G1 Synthetic documents only.** No real scan, contract or customer record enters the exercise. Tomas (DPO, A.5) confirms this before the first run, together with the checker-log terms in G4, with Leah as owner of A.7. `real_document_used` > 0 stops the exercise.
- **G2 Vendor terms.** Synthetic documents go to the vendor's service only if Priya (A.10) confirms the supplier terms allow it; otherwise an in-house stand-in is used and the report says so. The vendor's page-limit setting is never changed back to re-create n3; F1 is seeded in our harness.
- **G3 Isolation.** The mock record has no connection to the real record of decision or to any system that reads it. `live_system_touched` > 0 stops the exercise and returns it to Marcus.
- **G4 Checkers.** Checkers are volunteers, told the purpose (checking quality and time, not individual performance) and free to withdraw; the seeded faults are explained to them at the debrief. The timestamped action logs are personal data about the checkers: Tomas (DPO) confirms the basis and a retention period before the first run, the logs are pseudonymised at export, and employee representatives are informed or consulted where local employment rules require it for monitoring work. Results are reported per arm, never per person, and are never used in performance management. Arm K0 receives the walkthrough after the exercise.
- **G5 Publication.** Results go to the r8 review and to the person named to take the pilot gate, whatever they show. The simulation does not treat r8, does not reopen or re-close n3, does not replace the vendor evaluation on the 500-document sample, and does not settle the personal-data question.
- **G6 The film.** The film shows a recorded fault and a planned control. It must not be presented as evidence that the check works, that r8 is reduced or that the vendor's model is accurate. Phase 2 starts only after this script is reviewed and approved in issue #8, with the open points above answered or accepted by their owners.

### Real-world transfer

The same path would run every morning in the back office: a batch of scans, a service that reads them, a checker, and a record that later decisions rest on. What a board member, the owner or a checker should take away is that a bought model we cannot inspect can fail without saying so, as it did with long contracts in testing; that the check is the one place where a missing field can still be caught and challenged; and that the pilot should start only when the error review shows the check catches what the service misses, without costing more time than typing.
