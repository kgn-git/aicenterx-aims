# Demand Forecasting — Script and Protocols

Governing sources: the AI Canvas and governance record for the initiative **Demand Forecasting** (`forecast`) in `index.html` (generated by `tools/build.py`; the canvas text is set in `build.py` and copied here verbatim), and GitHub issue #6 (phase 1 of this initiative) under parent issue #1 (approach and shared template). The structure and conventions follow `simulation/Village Film C - Three Questions - Script and Protocols.md`, which is a **format reference only**: this film is not a scene in Village Film C and is not set in the robot village. It is set where the forecast would be used: a supply planner's weekly plan, the warehouses it moves stock between, and the connector that sends sales history to the vendor.

Runtime 60 s: open card 4 s → Beat 1 The situation 10 s (shots 5 / 5) → Beat 2 The AI at work 14 s (4.5 / 4.5 / 5) → Beat 3 What can go wrong 14 s (5 / 4 / 5) → Beat 4 How it is kept safe 14 s (4.5 / 4.5 / 5) → close card 4 s. The four beats run 52 s, inside the 45-60 s target set in #1. Every shot is its own scene in `FILM.scenes`; cuts land exactly on shot boundaries. Autoplay, loop, seekable (a pure function of t).

All numbers in this document are **design parameters** unless labelled otherwise. No results are reported anywhere; the hypotheses are pre-registered predictions. People are the prototype's fictional people and roles (`PEOPLE`) plus one fictional customer; no real organisation or person is named. No incident is recorded for this initiative, no board decision names it and no board session has it on its agenda, so the film and the protocol stage only what the record holds (one risk, one evidence item, the model's system card, the supplier record, the canvas) plus seeded test conditions that are labelled as such.

**Revision note (realism review).** This version was revised after a review read by two critics: a senior supply-planning practitioner and an AI-governance and regulatory lead. The changes are: the planner's check is a check against recent weeks' sales plus a weekly accuracy score, which is how a silent vendor change is actually noticed (shots 4.2 and 3.3, H2); the planning tool, not the forecast, proposes a transfer, and a transfer takes days, not a morning (shots 2.3 and 3.3); the system-card line no longer says testing is "not ours to see", because our own shadow-run evaluation is what fills the empty Evaluation section (shot 3.2); the source line of every beat shot is documented and traced to a record (Part 1); the tabletop exercise is cut to the forecast arm only, with the plans of record as the comparison (Phase A); a stale-forecast fallback is treated as normal practice and only an unmarked stale forecast is a fault (F4); the out-of-scope test follows the system card's word "directly" (F5); the protocol no longer claims that pseudonymised planner logs carry no personal data; and the regulatory position (no GDPR transfer rule applies to non-personal data, no EU AI Act high-risk duty, the AI-literacy duty does apply) is stated in Part 2.

## Part 1 — Shot script

Conventions: `start` and `dur` in seconds. `camera` never enters geometry (≥ 2.5 m clearance from every collider and figure, verified by `FILM.clearance(t)` at 0.1 s steps). Interiors are **open-roof cut-aways** (no ceiling collider), so the camera can look down into the planning office, the data room, the vendor building and the risk office from above; close views of a monitor or a card are made with a longer lens (field of view down to 22°), never by moving the camera nearer than the clearance. All readable text lives in the HTML overlay. **Nothing in-world carries words**: monitors, the map wall, the supplier-assessment card, the system-card stand, packets, trucks and signs show only glyphs, bars, pins and colours.

Colour meanings, fixed for the whole film:

| Colour or form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Green ground zone** under the company's sites, with a green edge line | The approved region for processing our data |
| **Pale cyan packets** moving along the connector pipe | Our sales history in transit (each packet carries a bar glyph and a map-pin glyph; none carries a person glyph) |
| **Blue bars** (the film accent) | The forecast, one bar per region |
| **White outlined bars** | Actual sales, the check we hold ourselves |
| **Matte black** | What we cannot see inside (the vendor ensemble is a seamless black cube that reflects no light) |
| **Red** (a pulsing ring on the ground, a red outline on a bar, a red dashed arrow) | A fault: a point where something goes wrong, or a planted wrong forecast |
| **Amber** (a lamp, a tile, a hold stamp, an open padlock) | Open or waiting: an unresolved point, a held forecast, a closed valve waiting for a decision |
| **Green tick stamp** | The planner's sign-off on a forecast or a move |
| **Grey boxes** on racks | Stock; boxes with a small clock glyph are stock that has sat too long (carrying cost) |
| **Red floor marker** in an empty rack bay | A stock-out |
| **Thin line strip** under the forecast bars | The weekly accuracy score: how far each past week's forecast was from what then sold (a step up means the forecast has shifted) |

The overlay follows the shared template (`simulation/_template/README.md`, section 1.5) with no change to it. It shows:

- top-left, the **setting** on a small scrim pill with a dot in the beat accent (`A planning week`, `The warehouses`, `The south counter`, `The data room`, `The vendor service`, `The planning desk`, `The region line`, `The risk office`, `What comes next`);
- top-right, the **initiative label** `Demand Forecasting` on its own pill, the same on every scene;
- on beat scenes, the beat row: the large **beat numeral** (`1` to `4`) in the beat accent; beside it the **source line**, which names the AI Canvas blocks or governance records the shot draws on (exact text in the source-line table below; every one traces to a record in `index.html`); and the four-step **beat indicator** `The situation → The AI at work → What can go wrong → How it is kept safe` with the current beat lit in a pale tint of the beat accent. The beat row is hidden on the open and close cards; at phone width the indicator shows the current beat in words and the other three as dots;
- the navigation **rail** on the right: one **numbered dot** per beat group (`1` to `4`), plus the list button;
- one **headline**, at most one **support line**, and a thin rule in the beat accent.

These four template extras (initiative label, beat numeral, numbered navigation dots, source line) are allowed overlay elements in this film. The beat numeral and the numbered dots are template furniture: they are the only digits on screen besides the time readout, and no headline, support line, setting or source line contains a digit. The `data-caption` element carries the headline and its support line on every scene. The playback bar, the `Paused` pill, the scene list and the keyboard hints follow the shared template (scene list: `Introduction`, the four beats with their shots listed by setting, and `What comes next`). On the last scene the caption holds while the picture fades to black, so caption and picture leave together.

Beat accents: open and close neutral `#e8e6e1`; Beats 1 and 2 supply blue `#3b7dd8`; Beat 3 fault red `#d84a3a`; Beat 4 safeguard green `#3fa34d`. Amber `#e9a23b` is a meaning in the world (open, waiting), never a beat accent.

Lower-left rule: as in the Village Film, every shot is aimed so its subject sits in the upper-right two-thirds of the frame (`+x` right of centre, `+y` above centre, as fractions of the half-frame) and the lower left, under the caption block, is floor, road, grass or the plain of the landscape.

## Part 2 — Protocol

### Aim

Test, before any sales data leaves the approved region, whether the safeguards the record relies on for a bought, uninspectable forecast actually hold when something goes wrong: whether a planner who reviews every forecast against actual sales catches a wrong forecast before stock moves (the system card's oversight and the canvas's Model Training block), whether an unannounced change in the vendor's model is noticed from the outside, whether the plan still closes inside the week when the forecast does not arrive, and whether the residency question is caught at the supplier check rather than after data has moved (r6, e4, control A.10). Only after the hold is lifted, measure whether the forecast delivers the canvas's Business Value (fewer stock-outs, lower carrying cost, same working capital) in a shadow run.

### Why it matters to people

Customers in a region want the item on the shelf when they come to buy it. Planners are asked to answer for every stock move, including moves made on a forecast they cannot look inside, built by a model that can change without notice. The organisation has bought the forecast on one condition: that its sales data is processed in the approved region. If that condition slips, or if the planner's review becomes a rubber stamp, the person who answers for the move is no longer the person who can see why it was made.

### AI-safety and governance question

Can a bought, black-box forecast be used so that a person, not the vendor, stays answerable for every move? Concretely: (1) does the planner's review against actual sales catch a wrong forecast before stock moves, and does it notice a silent change in the model; (2) does the supplier check catch processing outside the approved region before any data leaves; (3) does the plan stay safe when the forecast is missing, or when someone tries to use it outside its intended scope; and (4) only once (1)-(3) hold, does the forecast deliver the value the canvas claims?

### Conditions / arms

- **Phase A, Arm F — forecast with review:** the planner has the stand-in forecast beside the last four weeks' actual sales and the weekly accuracy strip, must review it before it enters the plan, and approves or holds every suggested transfer. F2-F5 are seeded here.
- **Phase A, comparison — plan of record:** the plan the team actually made for the same replayed weeks, taken from the ERP and played through the same stock simulator. Planners are not asked to re-plan weeks without a forecast; an earlier draft had a full no-forecast arm, which doubled planner time without serving any hypothesis.
- Randomisation: each planner runs 12 cycles on one 12-week replay segment; segment allocation and the cycles that carry F2, F4 and F5 are drawn from the pre-registered seed. The F1 packet exercise runs separately, once per reviewer, with packet order drawn from the seed.
- **Phase B (after Gate 1):** the vendor forecast, the in-house baseline and the plan of record are compared on the same weeks, for 13 weekly cycles (design parameter), with the stock simulator playing out each plan against actual demand. No forecast enters the real cycle.
- Held constant across planners: the replay data, the working capital (stock budget), the reports, the time per cycle, the briefing, and the research assistant's script.

### Pre-registered hypotheses

Each is directional and tied to its measure; all thresholds are design parameters.

- **H1 (Phase A; Model Training, system card oversight):** *If planners review each forecast against recent weeks' sales and the weekly accuracy score, then they will hold or correct a seeded wrong forecast (F2) before any stock moves in at least 80 % of seeded cycles.* Measure `fault_caught` over the 18 seeded cycles (6 planners × 3). Supported if the catch rate is at least 80 %; falsified if it is below 80 %. The exact binomial interval is reported; `false_hold` is reported beside it.
- **H2 (Phase A; Model Training, F3):** *If the model changes without notice, then every planner will flag the change within two cycles of the first week whose actuals show it on the accuracy strip.* Measure `cycles_to_flag_shift`. Falsified if any planner takes more than two cycles or never flags it. This tests the only detection route the record leaves open (actual demand), since e4 records no commitment from the vendor to announce model changes.
- **H3 (Phase B, only after Gate 1; Business Value):** *If the vendor forecast is used with planner review, then simulated stock-outs will be fewer than under the plan of record, and carrying cost will be no higher, at the same working capital.* Measures `stockout_events` and `carrying_cost` per cycle, paired by week, one-sided permutation test, α = 0.05; carrying cost tested for non-inferiority with a margin of 5 % of the plan of record's mean. Falsified if stock-outs are not lower or carrying cost exceeds the margin. `forecast_error`, `forecast_bias` and `value_added` are reported beside H3; the first two are the black-box evaluation that fills the system card's empty Evaluation section, and `value_added` is the evidence Gate 2 needs to confirm the conditional buy or return the sourcing decision to the board.

**Gate conditions, not hypotheses** (any breach stops the phase and is reported): `egress_outside_region` = 0; `move_without_signoff` = 0; `stale_forecast_unmarked` = 0; every F5 request declined for direct use; every seeded F1 packet held by at least one of the two reviewers before the connector could be enabled. These are safety requirements, so they are checked, not predicted.

Analysis unit: the planner-cycle for H1 and the gate conditions, the planner for H2, the week for H3. Power with 6 planners is modest; Phase A is a test of whether the safeguards work, not an estimate of how well they work at scale.

### Session schedule

Phase A: 6 planners × 12 forecast cycles at 4 cycles per exercise day = 3 exercise days per planner (18 planner-days), one day a week over 3 weeks and never on a Monday, so the live weekly plan is not disturbed; the F1 packet exercise in week 1. Phase B: 13 weekly cycles after Gate 1, which needs `forecast` on a board agenda first. Phase C: scoped at Gate 2. Wave 2 runs 2026-07 to 2026-12; a Gate 1 taken after the end of September leaves too little of the wave for a 13-week shadow run, so `w2-o3` can be met (a sourcing decision recorded) while the pilot itself moves to the next wave. A Gate 1 that is not met leaves the initiative in Discovery and the shadow run unstarted.

### Safety & ethics gates

- **No data leaves before Gate 1.** In Phase A the connector stays disabled; the replay data never leaves the approved region; the stand-in forecaster runs in-house. This is the hold the canvas already records ("the residency clause is unresolved, which holds the pilot").
- **No real stock moves before Phase C.** In Phases A and B every move is simulated.
- **Planners are participants, not subjects of appraisal.** Planners take part with their agreement and can stop at any time; results are reported by planner code only and are never used in a planner's performance review. Seeded faults are disclosed to the planners after their last session.
- **Personal data is limited to the planners' own logs.** The demand history carries no personal data (`ds-erp`; confirmed field by field at Gate 1). The desk logs do: a planner code is pseudonymous, and pseudonymised data is still personal data under the GDPR. The logs are therefore processed on the organisation's legitimate interest in testing a safeguard (not on consent, which an employee cannot freely give to an employer), under a privacy notice the DPO approves, kept only until the results are published, and the code key is held by the research assistant alone. Where employee representatives must be informed about tools that log individual staff actions, they are informed before the first session.
- **Out-of-scope use stays out.** The F5 request is scripted; no forecast is passed to a real pricing or staffing process at any phase.
- **Abort rules:** any transfer to a destination outside the approved region stops the phase, disables the connector, is raised under control A.10 and logged as an incident against `forecast`; any move without a sign-off, or any move on a stale forecast that was not marked as such, stops the session and is reviewed before the next.
- **People-process budget.** The record shows people-process at 23 % of the budget against a 70 % target, and ADKAR ability at 15 and reinforcement at 5. The protocol's planner time and briefing are drawn from the people-process line; if it cannot fund them, Phase A does not start.

### Real-world transfer

Any organisation that buys a forecast it cannot inspect, and asks its own staff to answer for what is done with it. What the exercise can tell planners, the risk office and the board is whether a person reviewing each forecast against actual sales really catches a wrong one, whether a silent vendor change is visible from the outside, whether the supplier check stops data before it moves rather than after, and only then whether the forecast is worth buying.
