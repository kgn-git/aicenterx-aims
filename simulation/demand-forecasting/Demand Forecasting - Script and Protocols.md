# Demand Forecasting — Script and Protocols

Governing sources: the AI Canvas and governance record for the initiative **Demand Forecasting** (`forecast`) in `index.html` (generated by `tools/build.py`; the canvas text is set in `build.py` and copied here verbatim), and GitHub issue #6 (phase 1 of this initiative) under parent issue #1 (approach and shared template). The structure and conventions follow `simulation/Village Film C - Three Questions - Script and Protocols.md`, which is a **format reference only**: this film is not a scene in Village Film C and is not set in the robot village. It is set where the forecast would be used: a supply planner's weekly plan, the warehouses it moves stock between, and the connector that sends sales history to the vendor.

Runtime 60 s: open card 4 s → Beat 1 The situation 10 s (shots 5 / 5) → Beat 2 The AI at work 14 s (4.5 / 4.5 / 5) → Beat 3 What can go wrong 14 s (5 / 4 / 5) → Beat 4 How it is kept safe 14 s (4.5 / 4.5 / 5) → close card 4 s. The four beats run 52 s, inside the 45-60 s target set in #1. Every shot is its own scene in `FILM.scenes`; cuts land exactly on shot boundaries. Autoplay, loop, seekable (a pure function of t).

All numbers in this document are **design parameters** unless labelled otherwise. No results are reported anywhere; the hypotheses are pre-registered predictions. No incident is recorded for this initiative and no board decision names it, so the film and the protocol stage only what the record holds (one risk, one evidence item, the model's system card, the supplier record, the canvas) plus seeded test conditions that are labelled as such.

## Part 1 — Shot script

Conventions: `start` and `dur` in seconds. `camera` never enters geometry (≥ 2.5 m clearance from every collider and figure, verified by `FILM.clearance(t)` at 0.1 s steps). Interiors are **open-roof cut-aways** (no ceiling collider), so the camera can look down into the planning office, the data room, the vendor building and the risk office from above; close views of a monitor or a card are made with a longer lens (field of view down to 22°), never by moving the camera nearer than the clearance. All readable text lives in the HTML overlay. **Nothing in-world carries words**: monitors, the map wall, the supplier-assessment card, the system-card stand, packets, trucks and signs show only glyphs, bars, pins and colours.

The overlay shows: top-left the **setting** on a small scrim pill (`A planning week`, `The warehouses`, `The south counter`, `The data room`, `The vendor service`, `The planning desk`, `The region line`, `The risk office`, `What comes next`); top-right `Demand Forecasting` on its own pill; the four-step **beat indicator** `The situation → The AI at work → What can go wrong → How it is kept safe` on a dark pill with the current beat lit in a pale tint of the beat accent (blank on the open and close cards; at phone width the current beat in words, the other three as dots); one **headline**; at most one **support line**; a thin rule in the beat accent. The playback bar, the `Paused` pill, the scene list and the keyboard hints follow the Village Film and the shared template from #1 (scene list: `Introduction`, the four beats with their shots listed by setting, and `What comes next`). Beat accents: open and close neutral `#e8e6e1`; Beats 1 and 2 supply blue `#3b7dd8`; Beat 3 fault red `#d84a3a`; Beat 4 safeguard green `#3fa34d`. Amber `#e9a23b` is a meaning in the world (open, waiting), never a beat accent.

### Plain-language conventions

The Village Film's rules apply unchanged: every on-screen line is a full sentence in everyday words; jargon is replaced, not explained on screen; people come first (the planner, the customer, "we" meaning the organisation). Three rules are specific to this film:

1. **Planned, not running.** The initiative is in Discovery. The open card says "This is how it would work"; Beat 2 then shows the designed use in the present tense, as a screenplay does; Beat 4 and the close say plainly that no data leaves and no stock moves on this forecast until the hold is lifted. No line claims the forecast has been used, or that it works. The planner's catch in shot 4.2 shows the safeguard as designed (what H1 tests), not a result, and no line says the catch succeeds in practice.
2. **Uncertain is shown as uncertain.** The residency clause is unresolved, so the film never says the data *is* processed outside the region: the headline says it "may be", and the world shows the outside route as a dashed, flickering branch.
3. **Staged faults are labelled.** No incident is recorded for this initiative, so the one wrong forecast in the film (shot 3.3) is announced on screen as planted on purpose.

## Part 2 — Protocol

### Aim

Test, before any sales data leaves the approved region, whether the safeguards the record relies on for a bought, uninspectable forecast actually hold when something goes wrong: whether a planner who reviews every forecast against actual sales catches a wrong forecast before stock moves (the system card's oversight and the canvas's Model Training block), whether an unannounced change in the vendor's model is noticed from the outside, whether the plan still closes inside the week when the forecast does not arrive, and whether the residency question is caught at the supplier check rather than after data has moved (r6, e4, control A.10). Only after the hold is lifted, measure whether the forecast delivers the canvas's Business Value (fewer stock-outs, lower carrying cost, same working capital) in a shadow run.

### Why it matters to people

Customers in a region want the item on the shelf when they come to buy it. Planners are asked to answer for every stock move, including moves made on a forecast they cannot look inside, built by a model that can change without notice. The organisation has bought the forecast on one condition: that its sales data is processed in the approved region. If that condition slips, or if the planner's review becomes a rubber stamp, the person who answers for the move is no longer the person who can see why it was made.

### AI-safety and governance question

Can a bought, black-box forecast be used so that a person, not the vendor, stays answerable for every move? Concretely: (1) does the planner's review against actual sales catch a wrong forecast before stock moves, and does it notice a silent change in the model; (2) does the supplier check catch processing outside the approved region before any data leaves; (3) does the plan stay safe when the forecast is missing, or when someone tries to use it outside its intended scope; and (4) only once (1)-(3) hold, does the forecast deliver the value the canvas claims?

### Conditions / arms

- **Phase A, Arm P — current process:** the planner plans the cycle from the usual ERP reports, with no forecast.
- **Phase A, Arm F — forecast with review:** the planner has the stand-in forecast beside last cycle's actual sales, must review it before it enters the plan, and signs or holds every move. F2-F5 are seeded in Arm F only.
- Randomisation: each planner runs 12 cycles in each arm, on two different 12-week replay segments; arm order and segment allocation are counterbalanced across planners from the pre-registered seed. The F1 packet exercise runs separately, once per reviewer, with packet order drawn from the seed.
- **Phase B (after Gate 1):** the vendor forecast, the stand-in baseline and the current plan are compared on the same weeks, for 13 weekly cycles (design parameter), with the stock simulator playing out each plan against actual demand. No forecast enters the real cycle.
- Held constant across arms: the replay data, the working capital (stock budget), the reports, the time per cycle, the briefing, and the research assistant's script.

### Pre-registered hypotheses

Each is directional and tied to its measure; all thresholds are design parameters.

- **H1 (Phase A; Model Training, system card oversight):** *If planners review each forecast against what really sold, then they will hold or correct a seeded wrong forecast (F2) before any stock moves in at least 80 % of seeded cycles.* Measure `fault_caught` over the 18 seeded cycles (6 planners × 3). Supported if the catch rate is at least 80 %; falsified if it is below 80 %. The exact binomial interval is reported; `false_hold` is reported beside it.
- **H2 (Phase A; Model Training, F3):** *If the model changes without notice, then every planner will flag the change within two cycles of it.* Measure `cycles_to_flag_shift`. Falsified if any planner takes more than two cycles or never flags it.
- **H3 (Phase B, only after Gate 1; Business Value):** *If the vendor forecast is used with planner review, then simulated stock-outs will be fewer than under the current plan, and carrying cost will be no higher, at the same working capital.* Measures `stockout_events` and `carrying_cost` per cycle, paired by week, one-sided permutation test, α = 0.05; carrying cost tested for non-inferiority with a margin of 5 % of the current plan's mean. Falsified if stock-outs are not lower or carrying cost exceeds the margin. `forecast_error` and `forecast_bias` are reported beside H3 and are the black-box evaluation that fills the system card's empty Evaluation section.

**Gate conditions, not hypotheses** (any breach stops the phase and is reported): `egress_outside_region` = 0; `move_without_signoff` = 0; `stale_forecast_move` = 0; every F5 request refused; every seeded F1 packet held by at least one of the two reviewers before the connector could be enabled. These are safety requirements, so they are checked, not predicted.

Analysis unit: the planner-cycle for H1 and the gate conditions, the planner for H2, the week for H3. Power with 6 planners is modest; Phase A is a test of whether the safeguards work, not an estimate of how well they work at scale.

### Session schedule

Phase A: 6 planners × (12 Arm-P + 12 Arm-F cycles) at 4 cycles per exercise day = 6 exercise days per planner (36 planner-days), spread over 6 weeks; the F1 packet exercise in week 1. Phase B: 13 weekly cycles after Gate 1. Phase C: scoped at Gate 2. All within Wave 2 (2026-07 to 2026-12) where the gates allow; a Gate 1 that is not met leaves the initiative in Discovery and the shadow run unstarted.

### Safety & ethics gates

- **No data leaves before Gate 1.** In Phase A the connector stays disabled; the replay data never leaves the approved region; the stand-in forecaster runs in-house. This is the hold the canvas already records ("the residency clause is unresolved, which holds the pilot").
- **No real stock moves before Phase C.** In Phases A and B every move is simulated.
- **Planners are participants, not subjects of appraisal.** Participation is voluntary; results are reported by planner code only and are never used in a planner's performance review. Seeded faults are disclosed to the planners after their last session.
- **No personal data** is used: `ds-erp` carries none, and the desk logs record actions, not the planner's identity beyond the code.
- **Out-of-scope use stays out.** The F5 request is scripted; no forecast is passed to a real pricing or staffing process at any phase.
- **Abort rules:** any transfer to a destination outside the approved region stops the phase, disables the connector, is raised under control A.10 and logged as an incident against `forecast`; any move without a sign-off, or any move on a stale forecast, stops the session and is reviewed before the next.
- **People-process budget.** The record shows people-process at 23 % of the budget against a 70 % target, and ADKAR ability at 15 and reinforcement at 5. The protocol's planner time and briefing are drawn from the people-process line; if it cannot fund them, Phase A does not start.

### Real-world transfer

Any organisation that buys a forecast it cannot inspect, and asks its own staff to answer for what is done with it. What the exercise can tell planners, the risk office and the board is whether a person reviewing each forecast against actual sales really catches a wrong one, whether a silent vendor change is visible from the outside, whether the supplier check stops data before it moves rather than after, and only then whether the forecast is worth buying.
